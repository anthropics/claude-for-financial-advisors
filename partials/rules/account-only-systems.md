# Systems that know accounts, not households

These rules cover a system whose records are accounts and the people or entities that own them. A custodian is one such system. It has no household object to look up.

**Find the household's accounts yourself, before the system's read goes out.** Search the system's client and account-owner records by the household members' names. Show the advisor every account the search returned, even one whose owner's name doesn't match. Give each one its owner's name, its masked account number, and the city and state the system gives. Then ask which accounts belong to this household. A read can't do this for you. Which accounts make up a household is the advisor's to say. A joint account, a trust account or an account under a maiden name can all belong to the household without a name match. This is the household question the skill already asks. When another question is open, ask this one in the same message.

**A name on an account is not always an owner.** Owner records can list people who have authority over an account but don't own it. An agent under a power of attorney is one example. A view-only contact is another. A search by name finds these people too. Where the system gives each person's role, list an account whose only match is such a person apart from the others. Put the role beside the name: "[name] is [role] on this account, not an owner". Never present that account as the household's own.

**A household grouping is a hint, not an answer.** Some systems group accounts into households of their own. An account in the same group as one the advisor confirmed can go on the list as a candidate. Label it with that grouping. The advisor still says which accounts belong.

**Take the confirmed set as given.** The read goes out with exactly the accounts the advisor confirmed. Its dispatch says the advisor confirmed them, and that a title that doesn't match the household's names is not a mismatch. Never add an account because its owner's name matches. Never drop one because its title looks different. Never widen a single account the advisor named to its household. Never narrow a household to one account. An account outside the confirmed set stays out of the household's figures and documents. It stays out even of a note saying it was left out. It may be another client's. The search may turn up an account with a tie to the household, such as one where a household member holds a power of attorney. Mention that account in your reply instead. Give the person's role. Ask whether the account belongs in this review. If the advisor says it does, it joins the confirmed set.

**Show account numbers masked.** Everything shown to the advisor or written into a document shows each account number masked. Never show one in full. A read needs a way to look each account up. It gets one in its dispatch. Where the system gives its own key for an account, that key goes in the dispatch. Otherwise the full number does.
